# Chapter 7: AI Tools for Product Managers

## Core Idea
"AI product management" (building AI-powered products) and "AI for product managers" (using AI tools to do the PM job better) are two distinct concepts that get conflated — this chapter is about the second: using AI to amplify your own PM craft, mapped stage-by-stage onto the AIPDL.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **AI Product Management vs. AI for Product Managers**: the conceptual split the chapter opens with.
  - When to use: whenever someone says "AI PM tools" ambiguously — clarify which meaning is intended.
  - How: **AI product management** = the craft of building products that are themselves powered by AI (deep collaboration with data scientists/ML engineers to integrate AI into the user experience — this is what Chapters 1-6 cover). **AI for product managers** (this chapter) = an AI-enhanced PM using AI tools to streamline their own workflow (market research, feedback analysis, prototyping), regardless of whether the underlying product uses AI at all.
- **AIPDL-Stage Tool Map** (Table 7-1): a reference mapping of tool categories to AIPDL stages — Nika is explicit this is a guideline, not a strict assignment, since most tools span multiple stages, and the tooling landscape moves fast enough that specific product names age quickly.
  - When to use: as a starting inventory when building your own AI-augmented PM workflow, understanding that specific vendor names will need refreshing over time.
  - How, by stage (illustrative tool names as of the book's writing — re-verify current options before recommending specific products): **Ideation** — brainstorming/note-organizing/deep-research tools. **Opportunity** — web-scraping and market/competitive-intelligence tools. **Concept/Prototype** — no-code builders, feedback-analysis tools, requirements-to-workflow converters. **Testing/Analysis** — transcription, user-behavior analytics, A/B testing platforms. **Rollout** — site builders, meeting-summarization assistants, presentation tools.
- **Collaboration & Tracking Tool Categories**: cross-functional coordination tools spanning the whole AIPDL (roadmapping, kanban/task tracking, engineering issue tracking, feedback-to-roadmap prioritization) — again, named examples will age; the categories are the durable part.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-enhanced PM**: the third AI PM category from Chapter 1 — uses AI tools in their own workflow, spans the entire product lifecycle (unlike AI builder/AI experiences PMs, who tend to follow a more sequential path).
- **Privacy/security caution with third-party AI tools**: explicitly flagged twice in the chapter — consult legal/privacy teams before feeding confidential product data into third-party AI tools, since many handle sensitive information.

## Mental Models
- Treat any specific tool name in this chapter as a placeholder for a *category* (e.g. "web-scraping competitive-intelligence tool," "requirements-to-workflow converter") — the AIPDL-stage mapping is the durable framework; the vendor names will need refreshing.
- Use the Karim Lakhani framing Nika opens with as your answer to "will AI replace PMs?": AI won't replace PMs, but AI-enhanced PMs will replace PMs who don't leverage AI.

## Anti-patterns
- **Feeding confidential product/company data into third-party AI tools without a privacy/legal check**: called out explicitly as a real risk, not a formality.
- **Conflating "AI product management" with "AI for product managers"**: leads to miscommunication about whether a discussion is about the product's AI or the PM's own tooling.
- **Treating the tool table as a fixed, permanent stack**: the chapter warns the tooling landscape changes extremely fast — workflows should evolve as new tools emerge, not calcify around a snapshot list.

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate "building an AI product" from "using AI to build products better" — they require different skills and different conversations with stakeholders.
2. Map your personal AI-tool stack against the AIPDL stages (ideation, opportunity, concept/prototype, testing/analysis, rollout) rather than picking tools ad hoc.
3. Treat cross-functional collaboration/tracking tools (roadmapping, kanban, issue tracking, feedback-prioritization) as spanning the whole lifecycle, not tied to one stage.
4. Always route third-party AI tool usage through your privacy/legal team before sharing confidential product or user data.
5. Expect your personal AI-PM workflow to keep evolving — this chapter is explicitly framed as a starting inventory, not a fixed system.
